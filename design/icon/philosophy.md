# Marginalia

**A design philosophy for the ExcellentPDF mark.**

Marginalia holds that the most honest image of reading is the moment a hand answers a page. The page is neutral,
quiet and almost architectural: a pale sheet, a folded corner, a few grey bars where words would be. Against it moves
one living gesture, a single stroke of deep ink that did not come from a press. The whole mark is the meeting of those
two things, the set and the written, and nothing else is admitted. Every part must look meticulously crafted, the
product of deep expertise and countless small corrections, never a quick arrangement of stock shapes.

Space is held by the sheet. Its proportions are a real page's, its corners softly turned, its fold cut at a true
diagonal and shaded as paper shades, lighter on the lifted face and darker in the crease. The grey lines of text sit on
a strict measure with a ragged right edge, as set type does, so that the stillness of the page reads at once. Restraint
here is not emptiness: it is the painstaking calibration of grey on white so that the page recedes and the ink speaks.

Colour is almost absent and therefore decisive. Paper is warm white, the type is cool grey, and the ink is one blue, a
deep iron-gall blue of the kind that dries darker than it was written. There is no gradient for its own sake and no
second accent. The ink's only modulation is the one a broad nib gives, thick where the pen moves across its edge and
thin where it moves along it, drawn with master-level care so that the stroke has the weight, entry and release of a
practised hand.

Rhythm comes from the stroke breaking its frame. The page is ordered and contained; the ink begins inside it and leaves
by the right edge, the way a note in a margin runs past the text block. That one transgression gives the mark its
motion and its scale: at full size it is a calligraphic flourish, at the smallest it is a single confident swash across
a white tile, and both must be recognisably the same gesture, each size drawn again by hand rather than shrunk.

Hierarchy is absolute: ink first, page second, everything else silent. No letters, no wordmark, no ornament inside the
icon; the sheet itself is the only typography. Craftsmanship is the whole argument. Pixel edges are placed, not
inherited; shadows are soft enough to lift the sheet but never to announce themselves; each rendering, from sixteen
pixels to two hundred and fifty-six, is finished as if it were the only one that would ever be seen.
