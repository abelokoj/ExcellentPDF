# Three movements for the ExcellentPDF mark

Three design philosophies, each a separate answer to the same question: what is the smallest image that holds a page,
a reader and a hand? Each was drawn again for every pixel grid from 16 to 1024 pixels, the product of painstaking
attention rather than a single drawing shrunk.

## I. Spread

Spread believes in the open book: two leaves lying in balance about a spine, the oldest picture of attention. The
silhouette is wide and symmetrical, and its symmetry is broken once, on purpose: the left leaf carries set lines of
type, the right leaf a single handwritten line, so that reading and writing face each other across the fold.

Form comes from curvature. Each leaf rises from the spine and settles toward its outer edge in one continuous arc, and
the arcs are mirrored to the pixel. Under the leaves a cover of deep teal shows only as a thin, even margin, like the
board of a well-bound notebook. A few hairlines at the outer foot give the block its thickness, and every one of them
is placed with master-level care.

Colour is held to three voices: ivory paper, teal board, and a vermilion ribbon falling from the head of the book past
its foot, ending in a clean swallow-tail. The ribbon is the mark's one bright note and its signature at every size;
at sixteen pixels it is the line that still says "a book, kept open at a place".

The craft is in restraint and calibration: the weight of grey in the type lines, the ribbon's exact width against the
spine, the shadow that lifts the book without announcing itself. It should look meticulously crafted, labored over by
someone at the top of the field.

## II. Dog-ear

Dog-ear is a monolith with one gesture. A single square of deep ultramarine, cut with soft corners, stands for every
page the reader will ever open; its top right corner is turned down, revealing pale paper on the back. Nothing else is
needed, and nothing else is admitted.

The fold is the whole drawing. Its diagonal is exact, its flap a true reflection of the corner it was, its crease
shaded as paper shades, lighter on the lifted face and darker in the hollow beneath. The colour of the square deepens
from ultramarine at its lit top to indigo at its foot, a slow and even gradient with no banding, the product of deep
expertise in how a field of colour holds light.

Scale is handled by subtraction. At display sizes, faint lines of type are pressed into the square, visible only as a
tone, so the monolith reads as a page; at taskbar sizes they vanish, and the square and its turned corner remain,
recognisable at a glance among any other icons.

Every edge sits on the pixel grid by intent. The work should feel inevitable, as if it could not be otherwise: the
painstaking result of countless small refinements to one shape.

## III. Spectrum

Spectrum treats colour as structure. Behind one white page, five sheets fan out like the leaves of a swatch book, each
in one colour of the app's own palette, each turned a few degrees further than the one before. The fan is an index of
modes, a hand of cards, a rainbow made orderly; it promises range without crowding.

Rhythm comes from equal steps. The sheets turn about a single point at the foot of the front page, by equal angles,
and their visible edges form a graduated band of colour whose intervals are measured, not guessed. Soft shadows
between the sheets give depth in thin layers, each laid with painstaking care.

The front page is quiet: ivory, softly rounded, carrying a few lines of grey type. It anchors the fan and keeps the
mark's promise that the document stays neutral while the colour lives around it.

At the smallest sizes the fan closes to three sheets so each colour keeps a whole pixel. The work should read as the
product of deep expertise in colour and spacing, calibrated so that every hue holds its own against both a light and
a dark taskbar.
